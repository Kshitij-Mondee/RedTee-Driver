# Hologram v2: what makes a projection believable, and what makes ours read as AI-made UI (2026-10-16)

Kshitij, 2026-10-16, after the wow pass: "the server rack depiction in hologram is very weird and doesn't look good.
Same with how language picks the next word. The style, the lines, the look - everything feels very basic and AI like.
Not something a real hologram would look like." and "Shouldn't look like AI made UI." On rendering: "Keep both, decide
wisely depending on the complexity. Things like bicycle, globes and things that can be visualized better in 3D: use
the WebGL 3D layer. For others, push the SVG harder for better results."

This note is a reference, not a checklist (CLAUDE.md rule 11). Nothing is copied from any studio. Direction
(storyboarding, pacing, registers) stays Loom's own; this is about the look. Sources were read in the browser only;
nothing was downloaded.

## 1. What the film and game FUI teams say (sources)

- **Every element has a job, or it goes.** For the Avengers HUD, stereo kept every element sharp and readable, so
  everything had to have a function and could not be decoration (Cantina Creative and Jayse Hansen;
  hudsandguis.com/2013/05/15/the-avengers). GMUNK on TRON: Legacy removed the greeble ("removing extra fluff") so each
  hologram read at a glance, even though the busier version looked better (pushing-pixels.org, GMUNK conversation,
  2011).
- **Negative space and darkness carry it.** Hansen was directed to make the Avengers glass screens mostly black so the
  faces behind read; he composes with the rule of thirds or the golden mean to steer the eye (pushing-pixels.org,
  Jayse Hansen conversation, 2012). Light only where the information is.
- **Depth is designed, not decorated.** Hansen varied depth between HUD layers to tell them apart, and anchored the
  picture plane carefully in stereo (same interview). Cantina built the Iron Man HUD in stereo passes, with reticles
  that move in from different depths and multilayered lighting and texture rendered in passes (cgw.com, "Cantina
  Creative Gives Iron Man 3 a Heads Up", 2013; provideocoalition.com on Iron Man 3).
- **Holograms are light sources.** On TRON: Legacy the holograms cast light on the actors, and because the plates were
  shot at f/1.4 the graphics were rendered with matching shallow depth of field (GMUNK, pushing-pixels.org).
  Cantina describes volumetric light projected into space and optical flares as core to the Iron Man 3 look.
- **Grounded in a real system, slightly ahead of it.** Hansen consulted an A-10 pilot and aimed a little ahead of real
  technology; real displays are overloaded and their users learn to read patterns at a glance rather than every value
  (pushing-pixels.org). Perception grounds Wakanda's technology in research on real phenomena (cymatics, ferrofluid)
  and designs for the expert character and for the viewer glancing at a phone (experienceperception.com; Fast Company
  on Black Panther; memberful.design on John LePore).
- **Physical, not pixel.** Territory Studio on Blade Runner 2049 wanted technology that felt advanced "but still
  physical": optical lenses shot on set for the morgue scan, macro photography of organic material, worn and glitchy
  screens for lower-status users (renderu.com, Territory's Peter Eszenyi; designweek.co.uk). Territory starts every
  project from scratch rather than from HUD templates (vfxvoice.com; schoolofmotion.com) and gives background screens
  close-up quality.
- **The trope is a trope.** Blue, transparent, monochrome, touch-driven holograms recur across Hollywood because they
  read as light on dark, not because of physics (summaries of "Why all of Hollywood UI looks the same", uxdesign.cc,
  and the Star Trek discussion it cites). Make It So (Shedroff and Noessel, Rosenfeld Media, 2012) treats volumetric
  projection as a pattern to study for what works with an audience, not to copy.

## 2. What real projected light looks like (sources)

- **Additive, never black.** A volumetric or light-field display cannot make black or an opaque surface: its voxels
  glow additively in a clear medium, so the image is translucent and dense areas drift toward white where layers
  overlap (blair-neal.gitbook.io, survey of alternative displays; US patent 11977244 on a rotating transparent-OLED
  volumetric display: "the color black cannot be actively generated"). So: overlaps brighten, back faces show through,
  and there are no dark fills inside the object.
- **Halation and bloom.** Bright light spreads into its neighbourhood: in film, blooming is scatter inside the emulsion
  and halation is light reflected back from the base, a soft halo around the brightest edges (photrio.com thread on
  blooming and halation). In practice it is emulated from a luminance key: only what is bright glows.
- **Edges brighter than faces (Fresnel).** Real-time hologram materials light a surface by how edge-on it is to the
  viewer and blend additively (godotshaders.com hologram and simple hologram shaders; ektogamat's
  threejs-vanilla-holographic-material). A projected shell reads as rims and silhouettes, with faint faces.
- **Instability.** A projection is never perfectly stable: a faint flicker, thin scan lines or interlace, and a slight
  red and blue split at the edges, all subtle (filmora hologram guide; diyphotography Photoshop hologram guide; the
  Blender chromatic aberration note on blendernation.com: a lens artefact to match when compositing CG).
- **Falloff and fog.** Far parts are dimmer; light falls off from the emitter (the pad); a beam is visible only where
  there is something in the air to scatter it (dust, haze).
- **It lights its surroundings.** The pad, the floor and nearby surfaces pick up its colour (GMUNK; the Photoshop guide
  adds glow to nearby surfaces).

## 3. The tells: why our frames read as AI-made UI

Looked at in `2026-10-16-style-stress/out_hologram_after/frames/` and `out_hologram_wow/frames/`:

| tell | where | why it reads as generated |
|---|---|---|
| corner brackets on every box | every panel, card, flow step, datum, chrome | a single ornament repeated everywhere is a template, not a design |
| one uniform cyan at one weight | lines, text, rings, mesh, wireframe | real light varies in intensity: hot cores, dim fills, a falloff |
| a generic hex grid behind everything | the field | decoration with no job (the opposite of the FUI rule) |
| mono caps tracked wide everywhere | kickers, tags, panel titles, chrome | one typographic voice; no hierarchy between a reading and a label |
| symmetric, equal-weight panels | stress frames 1 and 2 | a dashboard grid; nothing leads the eye |
| no light behaviour | everything | no bloom from bright cores, no halation, no falloff, no light on the pad or floor, flat fills |
| no imperfection | everything | perfect vectors with no scan texture, flicker or fringe read as a screenshot, not a projection |
| data that looks invented | round values, a hexagon per token, "LAYER 1/2/3" pills | real measurements have precision, history and units; real model internals are not hexagons |
| wireframes that are grids on boxes | the SVG rack | a box with face grids is a diagram of a box; a real hologram shows the object's own edges and silhouette |
| the LLM explainer as nodes in boxes | stress frame 2 | a hexagon lattice in three labelled pills is a stock "neural net" picture, not attention |

## 4. Principles for Loom's hologram v2 (ours, from the above)

1. **Light is the medium.** Additive colour; no dark fills inside a projected object; brightness says importance
   (a hot core, a dim body, near-black elsewhere). Bloom only from what is bright; never on words.
2. **Edges and rims, not grids.** A projected object shows its silhouette and real edges (Fresnel rims), faint faces,
   and its own detail; never a grid drawn on a box. Complex objects (a bicycle, a globe, a rack) are true 3D (WebGL);
   diagrams stay SVG.
3. **A little imperfection, deterministic.** Scan bands moving through the object, a fine interlace, a faint flicker
   per frame from a seeded hash, a slight red and blue fringe at bright edges. Subtle enough to read past.
4. **Depth by light and focus.** Far layers dimmer and softer (depth of field); near layers crisp; overlaps brighten.
5. **The emitter is real.** A pad that lights the object from below, a beam that is visible only through haze and
   motes, a pool of light on the floor.
6. **Typography with hierarchy.** One reading (a value, a word) is large and plain; labels are small sentence case;
   micro detail (an index, a unit, a precision, a time) is smaller and dimmer. Mono only for machine output.
7. **Purposeful asymmetry.** One subject leads; supporting readings orbit it at different depths. No equal-weight grid.
8. **Ornament earns its place.** No brackets on every box: a plane has a lit edge where the light catches it; a lock
   brackets one thing at one moment.
9. **Data looks measured.** Values with the precision the source has, a little history (a trace), a unit, a time; in a
   lesson every number still comes from the knowledge sheet (rule 1).
10. **Readability gates stay.** Words on plates or clear field, at or above the caption floor, never inside a glow, a
    blur or a fringe.
