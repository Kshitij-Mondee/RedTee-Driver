# Style guide (corpus, 96 videos, 6307 shots)

## House constants
- Format: a 16:9 film (analysed at 854x480), one narrated lesson of about 4 to 10 minutes; the code's durations run 252 to 587 s with batch medians of 315 to 363 s. (95 videos; exceptions 1)
- Silent branded open: no speech for about the first 6 s while a near-white card with the red brand mark plays; the code puts the first spoken word at 5.97 to 8.69 s (11.98 s in one film). The picture speaks first. (84 videos; exceptions 12)
- The brand card cross-dissolves into a centred, attributed one-line quote card in thin grey type on heavy negative space (quote text about 0.06 to 0.10 of frame height), held long enough to read before the first picture. (74 videos; exceptions 22)
- Close on one held final image (a quiet tableau, a small gag or a call-to-action card) while credits roll in a thin solid bar along the bottom edge for about 6 to 20 s; no recap slide. The closing image is often a rotating studio outro in the house red. (96 videos; exceptions 0)
- One saturated accent hue is reserved for the element the narration is naming, against a muted, desaturated or single-hue field; every whole-film record states it, and colour accent is the most frequent focal cue in every batch that counted it (for example 330 of 702 and 375 of 1003 shots). Whether it is exactly one accent or one dominant accent is unmeasured. (96 videos; exceptions 0)
- The camera is locked by default (static in 5,158 of 6,307 shot records, sub-shots included); life comes from motion inside the drawing, and camera moves are kept for reveals of scale, entering a space or landing a word. (96 videos; exceptions 0)
- Hard cuts on the narration beat are the default join (about 64 to 79 percent of joins per batch, counts that include some sub-shot boundaries, which are not real joins); gradual joins (morph, zoom-through, carried object, material wipe, dissolve) are kept for a change of idea, time, place or scale. (81 videos; exceptions 3)
- One off-screen narrator carries the film at a measured pace (about 112 to 185 words per minute, batch medians 129 to 161); no on-screen host, and animated characters do not speak on screen. (84 videos; exceptions 12)
- New picture elements arrive when they are named: the vision model tagged picture and speech as coinciding in 4,810 of 5,239 shots in the seven batches that counted it. This is the model's reading at about 1 fps, not a measured offset. (84 videos; exceptions 0)
- Textured flat: flat fills dressed with a paper, grain, brush, halftone or charcoal surface over the whole film rather than clean digital vector. How much of the grain is artwork and how much is 480p compression is unsettled. (45 videos; exceptions 3)
- Drawn flat 2D illustration is the base layer; 3D renders, photographs, clay or live footage enter as short framed inserts or register breaks held to the same palette and finish. (30 videos; exceptions 6)
- A few linked worlds per film (a story world, a diagram or interior world, an archive), tied by one palette and finish, a recurring token or a hub set rather than one continuous place. The records give this label to nearly every film, so it may be a default. (65 videos; exceptions 7)
- On-screen text is short and functional: labels on objects, numbers, signage, single stamped keywords and term cards; no narration sentences apart from the opening quote, and list text appears in only a handful of shots per batch. (45 videos; exceptions 3)
- Something moves in most seconds (motion share per film about 0.61 to 0.90): held frames are kept alive by one slow ambient loop (flicker, drift, sway, median about 4.5 s) while content motion carries meaning. (36 videos; exceptions 0)
- Characters are reduced and few (median one per shot, p90 three): minimal faces, emotion carried by pose or one feature, and no animated lip sync; the only lip-sync shots are live speakers. (22 videos; exceptions 2)

## Style families
- **Dark void, near monochrome, one reserved light or red** (10 videos; closest pack cinematic-noir): near-black, slate or night field as the stage; a place is drawn only when the story names it; near-monochrome figures (white line, pale fill or dark silhouette) with one reserved hue: red for danger or death, acid green or cyan for the supernatural or the protagonist, gold for value; light drawn as a soft radial pool, halo or lamp cone isolating one subject; no cast shadows; heavy grain, stipple, charcoal or crack texture over every layer, type included; crowds and threats as flat dark silhouettes or one repeated glyph, sometimes with only glowing eyes; minimal or exaggerated single-feature faces; emotion carried by pose and colour wash; joins by spreading black shapes, irises, collapse to a point, inversions and ribbon morphs; pulsing rhythm: 1 to 4 s cuts for conflict, long near-still holds for emotion. Loom lacks: charcoal, chalk, crack and stipple textures that sit on every layer including type (painterly finish is listed as not possible); an accent-lock rule: one hue keeps one meaning for the whole film, checked per frame; a void wipe: a black shape that spreads from inside the picture; an inverted line-art mode (white figures with dark outline on black); a true path morph for glow-thread and ribbon chains (sun to hand to eye); a shatter primitive for hold-then-snap beats; grain judged on the encoded MP4 at visible strength (finish is near off by default)
- **Brush, ink and watercolour wash on paper** (6 videos; closest pack editorial-paper): cream or aged paper ground on every shot; dissolves pass through blank paper so the ground never breaks; loose ink wash or watercolour for worlds and crowds, crisper line or outline-free blocks for the focal figure; one warm accent (red sun, gold line, red thread, one flower) per shot on a muted wash; hand-brushed or typewriter lettering for names, dates and measurements, drawn on as spoken; tiny repeated silhouettes for crowds and scale in vast negative space; background hue washes or cycles while the subject holds, to show time or mood; slow constant pans and parallax over still paintings; scenes slide off and rise in. Loom lacks: painterly ink-wash, brush and watercolour fills (not possible today; imported assets only); brush lettering with variable stroke width as a draw-on; feathered soft shape edges; detailed illustrated backgrounds at this density; organic pose morphs of one blob body; a timed background palette cycle as a pack parameter
- **Loose single-stroke ink or pencil line on off-white paper, redrawn in place** (9 videos; closest pack line-art): loose single-stroke line with visible overdraw or hatching; no clean vector edge; one off-white, cream or cold grey paper field that never changes, often with a soft vignette; near-monochrome grey line with at most one warm accent wash, on the named object; rounded minimal-face or faceless figures; posture carries the emotion; background architecture and extras drawn in a lighter ghosted line; ideas arrive as small vignettes on the one field, joined by redraw in place or short dissolves; few hard cuts; hand-lettered labels in the same hand, written on with the keyword, crossed out when superseded; numbers carried by signs, tags and counters inside the drawing. Loom lacks: frame-to-frame line boil (not possible); a static seeded wobble exists only as the whiteboard pack's rough.js sketch mode, with hachure fills and a white board rather than paper; organic redraw between two drawings; only cross-fade or hold-and-transform of vector vignettes is possible; a hand-lettered font that matches the stroke, as a pack font role; a faceless figure rig (dot eyes, no mouth) whose acting is posture only; per-layer line weight and opacity so the background reads as ghosted; pencil hatching and wash fills inside line art (line-art keeps colour in the lines only); a cut detector and pacing model that understand redraw-in-place films
- **Wobbly hand outline over flat colour under grain or paper** (12 videos; closest pack story-cartoon): thin to thick, slightly irregular dark hand outline over flat fills; paper grain, crayon, speckle or film noise with a soft vignette over the whole frame; warm cream or one saturated field per idea, with a grainy near-black mode for the mind, the night or the unknown; dot-eye, one-line-mouth figures and personified objects; emotion read from pose; one colour bound to one concept for the whole film (red threat, cyan imaginary); labels on paper tags, strips and letter tiles; diagrams of thin lines, nodes, insets and rows of identical figures; state shown by colour swaps on an unchanged layout; the frame clears to an empty field between ideas; disembodied or giant hands present, move and remove objects. Loom lacks: an irregular hand outline as a static stroke parameter inside an outlined pack (story-cartoon's ink line is even); grain, crayon and vignette finish on at visible strength, judged on the encoded MP4; a hand rig or prop family (grip, present, release); a magnifier inset with leader line that cross-fades sub-views; a seeded single-hue scribble-field background generator; path-to-path shape morphing between compositions; a concept-to-colour map that a check can enforce
- **Uniform dark outline storybook on muted parchment and earth fields** (10 videos; closest pack story-cartoon): flat cel fills with a uniform thick or thin dark outline on every figure and prop; desaturated parchment, sepia, olive, sage or grey-blue fields, often with paper grain and a corner vignette; period or institutional cast (courts, halls, ships, monasteries) with one stern pose or gesture each, or 2 to 3 stepped expressions; illustrated places in 2 to 3 depth planes, backgrounds hazy or outline-free; one saturated accent per shot or per character on the narrated element; torn-edge label cards, stamped years and verdict stamps; hand-drawn maps as flat stages; crowds as rows of near-identical figures or black silhouettes; quantities as repeated icons or objects; ornamental borders and pattern bands in some members. Loom lacks: period and institutional place sets (hall, courtroom, kitchen, ship deck, street); Loom places are office, meeting room, server room, warehouse and body; a larger cast of distinct outlined characters with fixed accents (needs imported character assets); a per-layer outline policy: outlined figures over un-outlined hazy backgrounds; engraving and old-map textures, ornamental borders and pattern-band fills; page-turn and scroll-unroll joins; richer walk, run and full-body acting (basic today)
- **Thin-outline rounded cartoon on single flat or soft gradient fields** (11 videos; closest pack story-cartoon): thin dark outlines, rounded blob bodies, big heads, dot eyes and blush circles; one flat saturated, candy, pastel or two-stop gradient field per scene, often with no horizon; invisible agents (microbes, signals, feelings) given a face and tracked in one colour all film; a saturated interior world (inside the body) or a black void with one glowing subject for the mind; hand-lettered labels and badges with offset shadows that pop on the word and clear; giant numbers and icon grids for statistics, with a figure acting the number; diagrams drawn onto the landscape or as concentric-band cutaways with one leader line; small ambient loops (petals, doodles, sway) on every held shot. Loom lacks: a blank or dot-eyed rounded figure style for rigs (current rigs carry detailed faces); a persistent personified agent family (microbe, defender, object mascot) with face and limbs; a soft halo bloom tied to character state; circular portal masks for zoom-through joins and scalloped or material-shaped wipe edges; a held low-opacity ghost dissolve that the double-exposure check accepts as deliberate; a palette restraint option: candy saturation can read juvenile to employees; a closed-outline shape morph
- **Outline-free geometric primitives on one solid field per beat** (4 videos; closest pack presenter-pop): flat fills, no outline and no texture or only a faint one; every idea built from dots, circles, pills, trapezoids and thin lines; faces as two arcs and two dots or none; one solid saturated field per beat or topic; the whole field changes hue at section boundaries; one warm accent (usually red) on the active element; red wrong or active, green right or adopted; in-place morphs where one shape carries several meanings; joins by scale-to-fill, growing circle, rising panel, collapse to a point and zoom-through; serif or bold sans words used as the picture; equal panels at identical scale for comparisons; scenes framed by ovals, book pages or cards on a contrasting field. Loom lacks: true shape-to-shape path morphing between arbitrary primitives; a scale-to-frame-edge join where the outgoing shape becomes the incoming field; a per-beat field colour sequence token in the pack; a primitive-only vocabulary lock that refuses non-primitive props; oval and book-page vignette masks; multiply-blend overlapping circles as a builder
- **Outline-free value-separated flat with fine grain, science and documentary** (7 videos; closest pack editorial-paper): shapes separated by value or a soft glow halo, no outlines (thin lines only for faces); fine uniform grain or paper noise over every flat fill; muted cool fields (teal, grey, navy) or a two-hue saturated field, with one reserved warm accent; faceted angular planes for faces and terrain in some members; magnifier insets and zoom-throughs from body or landscape to micro scale; a dark navy mode for the micro and the unknown; a small reusable cast of agents or personified organs; a recurring anchor set (bench, presenter beside a diagram); everyday props (warning signs, tape measures, containers) carry the quantities; state changes as discrete swaps on the keyword; slow push-ins and pull-backs as reveals. Loom lacks: a faceted (planar) shading mode for faces and rock; a magnifier inset with pointer tail as a component; grain at visible strength judged on the encoded MP4; a personified-object face kit (neutral, strained, relieved); courtroom and news-desk sets; particle palettes as a pack parameter
- **Cut paper, torn edges and collage of physical material** (8 videos; closest pack editorial-paper): torn, scalloped or hand-cut paper edges and dry-brush or crumpled grounds; no outlines; faceless cutout figures with identity by one clothing colour; soft drop shadows on paper shapes; archival photographs, paintings and photo-cutout heads set in frames with label plates (some members); torn-paper label bands, typewriter or monospace captions, starbursts and hexagon callouts as the annotation layer; transitions made of the material itself: torn-edge wipes, page turns, a hand carrying an object, smoke, scraps; one physical token (coin, boat, book, letter) carried through the argument and transforming into the next scene; halftone dot shading on 3D or flat fills in some members; clay figures and photographed props in one. Loom lacks: torn, scalloped and crumpled edge masks for shapes, wipes and labels; a halftone shading and halo finish; an intake path for licensed archival photos, paintings and photo cutouts; sticker outline plus hard offset shadow as a pack treatment; page-turn, paper-corner peel and paper-scrap dissolve joins; clay and stop-motion material (not reachable in code; needs filmed or rendered assets)
- **Print and comic: halftone, cross-hatch and panels** (3 videos; closest pack story-cartoon): halftone dots or cross-hatching instead of gradients; bold or thin consistent dark outlines with flat two-tone fills; comic panels, covers, speech bubbles, onomatopoeia and starburst emphasis; a dot field as the medium pictures form from and dissolve into (one member); two-pole colour coding held all film, or era shifts by palette (monochrome past, saturated present); large serif or corner year tags as the timeline spine; glowing data numerals and ragged ink-blot vignettes in one member. Loom lacks: halftone and cross-hatch shading modes; a particle-to-shape sampler so a dot field condenses into a portrait or object and back; comic panel, cover and speech-burst templates; a ragged ink-blot vignette mask; field lines synced to moving sources in the SVG runtime (possible only as a Manim clip); a page-turn join
- **Dark-field glow lines, isometric and soft 3D for mechanisms** (6 videos; closest pack glow-flat): dark blue, violet or teal field with emissive glow lines, rings and travelling dots for energy and data; outline-free flat or soft-gradient shapes; isometric blocks or soft glossy and matte 3D forms in some members; one warm accent (or one magenta) on the element being explained, on a cool field; one centred circular object or symmetric channel as the spine of many shots; labels in staggered waves with thin leader lines; counters landing on the spoken number; the object transforms across joins; camera pushes through openings or pulls out from one unit to the field; flat human scenes as anchors between technical voids; grey blueprint grids and exploded views appear as a register in other families. Loom lacks: a lit isometric board or room-in-void diorama as a ready place (the iso kit exists, the sets do not); an organic tunnel builder and camera fly-through join for three.js clips; soft clay and glossy wet materials for 3D clips; radial dial and meshing gear-train builders; a motion-blur whip join and radial zoom blur; an exploded-view parameter on iso objects
- **Live speakers inside flat collage, badges and doodle overlays** (10 videos; closest pack presenter-pop): locked-off centred live talking heads or a stage speaker, graded toward grey, duotone or the illustration palette; an illustrated studio set or circle mask whose monitor hosts the footage, as bookend and portal; hand-drawn starburst name badges in a lower corner; saturated flat doodles, props and keywords popped over the footage on the word, never over the face, cleared at once; torn-paper collage cards and hand-lettered title cards between testimony holds; first word inside about 1.6 s; faster speech and several speakers; long talking-head holds of about 11 to 18 s. Loom lacks: a video-footage layer with grade, duotone and mask controls (not in the capability list); intake for consented client interview footage; an overlay layer that tracks a filmed speaker and keeps clear of the face; a starburst badge plate family for names and roles; hand-lettered display fonts and torn-paper card templates; talking-head pacing rules (long holds) in the storyboard model

## Techniques
- **One saturated accent on the narrated element** `single_accent_on_muted_field` [colour, 96 videos, Loom: needs_param]: The frame sits in one muted, desaturated or single-hue family and exactly one element, the one the narration names in the current clause, carries a saturated hue. When the narration moves to a new subject the accent moves with it and the previous element is demoted back to the field family. When: Every explanatory frame in every style of the corpus: it tells the learner where to look without arrows or labels, and matters most in busy places, crowds and multi-part diagrams where only one part is being discussed. Timing: Persistent through each shot; accent moves observed at colour_change medians of 0.6 to 1.1 s across batches. Batch counts: colour accent was the focal cue in 214 of 640 shots (batch 01) and 375 of 1003 (batch 08).
- **One small ambient loop keeps every hold alive** `ambient_life_on_holds` [motion, 94 videos, Loom: exists]: On a held frame the subject and camera stay still while one small, slow, low-contrast loop (rain streaks, drifting specks, smoke, flame flicker, steam, a sway, a blink, a slowly turning ring) keeps the frame from freezing without competing with the content. When: Every hold longer than about 2 to 3 s: diagrams being read, portraits, establishing shots, emotional and philosophical beats, term cards, end cards. Timing: Ambient loop medians 3.0 to 5.2 s across the eight batches (p25 about 1.6 to 2.75 s, p90 7.8 to 10.4 s); secondary motion medians 0.29 to 0.5 s.
- **Credits in a black bottom bar over a held final image** `credits_bar_over_held_image` [ending, 84 videos, Loom: needs_param]: The film ends on one calm, sparse final image (a tableau, a map with a tiny traveller, a procession, the opening studio) that holds with only small ambient or character loops while a thin solid black bar of credits sits along the bottom; the picture never cuts away to a separate credits card. When: Every lesson end: close on a calm, readable image while credits, source notes or citations run. Timing: Credits run about 6 to 20 s across the films (most 8 to 19 s); credit text height median 0.03 of frame (p90 0.05, code).
- **The picture changes on the word that names it** `visual_event_lands_on_spoken_word` [sync, 81 videos, Loom: exists]: Every new element (label, icon, figure, number, prop) enters as its naming word is spoken, and each shot's one main visual event (a morph, a landing, a break, a glow, a gesture) completes or peaks on the key word that names the result, so picture and word confirm each other. Nothing appears before it is named. When: Always: every beat, every list item, every diagram part, every cause-and-effect result. It is the main tie between picture and narration across the corpus. Timing: Entrances are short: pop-in medians 0.4 to 0.58 s across batches (p25 about 0.3 s, p75 up to 1.38 s), scale-in 0.67 to 0.88 s, slide-in 0.75 s, fade-in 0.5 to 0.7 s; morph and transform-into medians about 1.0 to 1.3 s. The vision model labelled the large majority of shots as picture and word coinciding and described entries starting slightly ahead of the word. That is the model's description, not a measurement (its offset field reads 0.0 for nearly every shot), so no offset value is given here; set any lead by eye on the strip.
- **Silent brand card dissolving into a quote card** `brand_quote_cold_open` [opening, 74 videos, Loom: exists]: The film opens with no voice on a near-white field: a centred wordmark with one saturated accent holds, cross-dissolves on the same axis into a centred one or two line quote in thin type with a much smaller attribution, then hands off to the first scene. The quote names the film's theme, tension or paradox before any picture or term. When: Any lesson opener that should set a calm, credible, editorial register and pose the central tension in one line before content starts. Timing: The card shot spans 0 to about 6.0 s in most films (6.0 to 6.8 s; 10.4 s in one film that adds a year stamp and a character card); the wordmark holds about 1 to 1.2 s before the dissolve; first spoken word at about 6 to 8.7 s in most films (code word timings), with a few outliers near 12 to 13.7 s; brand title about 0.15 to 0.20 of frame height on the brand beat.
- **Zoom through a portal shape into the next scene** `zoom_through_portal` [transition, 64 videos, Loom: exists]: The camera pushes into an aperture that already exists in the outgoing frame (a monitor, a doorway, a letter's counter, a map pin or dot, an eye, a mouth, a liquid surface, a cell boundary, a comic burst) until it fills the frame and becomes the next scene, so the change reads as travel through one continuous space and the join means 'into'. When: Changes of scale or register: outside to inside, system to part, body to cell, map to place, photo to memory, set to presenter, device to its content. Timing: Portal shots are usually short, about 0.6 to 3.6 s (ease-in moves median about 0.8 s); slow dives such as organ to cell run up to about 7 to 14 s.
- **Locked camera by default; one move with one meaning when it moves** `static_camera_default` [camera, 60 videos, Loom: exists]: The frame is locked in most shots and the motion budget goes to the content: morphs, stamps, draw-ons, particles, gestures and ambient loops supply all the movement, and rhythm comes from cuts and shot-size changes. When the camera does move, it is one slow move with one meaning (push in to emphasise or enter, pull out to reveal scale, tilt to reveal a destination, truck to follow a route), never coverage and never compound. When: Default for every explanatory, diagram and character shot; reserve lens moves for reveals, entries, descents and scale changes. Timing: Static camera in about 0.8 of shot records in the four batches that counted it (512 of 640, 529 of 621, 546 of 702, 815 of 945); per-film motion share still high (about 0.59 to 0.98). Eased camera moves: ease-in-out median about 0.4 s, ease-out about 0.75 s, ease-in about 0.83 s; reveals in the records run 1 to 9 s.
- **One paper, grain or texture finish over everything** `film_wide_texture_finish` [world_building, 60 videos, Loom: needs_param]: One fine paper grain, film grain, noise, stipple, crayon, watercolour, halftone or cracked-paint finish sits over every fill and layer, backgrounds, diagrams and inserts included, often with a soft darkening edge vignette, so flat vector, 3D and type read as one printed or hand-made material and hard cuts read like pages of one sketchbook. When: Every editorial, storybook, sketch or textured flat style; films that mix 2D, 3D and photo; flat noir that would otherwise look like a sterile digital void. Timing: Constant across the film. Texture coded paper in 212 shots across 10 films of one batch; another batch coded texture none in 456 of 1003 shots, so the finish is common but not universal.
- **Pull back from one detail to reveal scale or context** `pull_back_scale_reveal` [camera, 54 videos, Loom: exists]: One continuous pull-out starts tight on a highlighted unit or ambiguous detail (a tile, a rack, a figure, a child on a rope, a flat bar) and reveals it as one of many, or as the surface of something larger (a fleet, a lattice, a crowd, a city, a globe); the subjects hold still so the wide reads as a reframe of the same world, not a new scene, often with a tiny human for scale. When: Scale and context: one case to a population, a local event becoming system-wide, showing one part as a piece of a larger system, surprise reframes, emotional payoffs. Timing: Reveal shots run about 0.8 to 18 s (fast reveals 0.8 to 1.4 s; slow pulls 4 to 13 s, one continuous pull of 11.4 s); ease-out moves median about 0.9 s (p90 4.8 s). Pull-out is tagged in every batch.
- **Frame within the frame** `frame_within_frame` [composition, 50 videos, Loom: needs_param]: An aperture native to the world (doorway, window, arch, mirror, monitor, magnifier rim, gilt frame, coin, scroll, car mirror, foliage) frames the subject, isolating it, marking it as observed, reported or entered, or hosting another world inside this one. The content inside can change while the surround stays still, and an inset lens can follow a moving subject. When: Thresholds and arrivals, points of view, quoted evidence or a backstory, memories, the boundary between two domains, singling one item out of a crowd, authority behind a barrier. Timing: Static device held for the whole shot (shots of 1.4 to 9.6 s); an arrival in a doorway observed landing on the spoken name in a 3.0 s shot; a following lens moves with path-follow (median 1.7 s). Tagged in 30 and 42 shots across 9 and 8 films of two batches.
- **Slow push-in that lands on the named subject** `push_in_on_named_subject` [camera, 48 videos, Loom: exists]: A slow push-in over a held picture isolates one subject from a busy field, tightens on a face or symbol, and arrives as the key word or name is spoken, adding weight without adding information; a label may land once the object fills the frame, a very slow push on a nearly still figure builds unease or inward focus, and a sub-second push makes a reaction punch. When: Emphasising one element of a held scene, naming one item among many, introducing a protagonist, building tension before a reveal, a legacy line or an inward turn. Timing: Push-ins sit in shots of about 1 to 15 s, with very slow tension pushes of 9 to 17.6 s; a single-out push about 1 s; a reaction push 0.6 s. Push-in is tagged in 40 to 57 shots per batch and is the most common move in several batches.
- **Music alone carries the branded open, credits and wordless beats** `music_carries_silent_bookends` [sound, 47 videos, Loom: exists]: The film opens on the brand mark and a quote or title card with no speech; the music bed carries those first seconds, the narrator's first word lands after the card, on or after the first picture, and music alone returns under the credits and any wordless release beat. When: Every film open and close, cold opens and emotional pauses; it sets a calm, premium register before content. Timing: First word at about 6.3 to 8.1 s in every film of the batches that measured it (batch 01: ten films at 6.5 to 7.3 s and one at 16.05 s; batches 05 and 06: all 12 films each); code bed level a median 5.6 to 6.8 dB below speech while the voice runs. Batch 08 tagged 56 of 1003 shots as no speech.
- **Hold at anchors, cut fast through lists** `hold_anchors_cut_lists_fast` [pacing, 46 videos, Loom: exists]: Rhythm follows meaning, not a fixed shot length: the film slows to long, nearly still holds at definitions, key lines, results, data builds and diagrams, and speeds to short shots through lists, alarms and examples, then returns to a hold. When: Every lesson. The burst serves listing and urgency, the hold serves understanding; diagrams with more than one step or layer get the longest shots. Timing: Shot length batch medians 4.2 to 5.0 s (p25 2.4 to 2.6 s, p75 8.2 to 10.4 s, p90 8.4 to 14.8 s); cuts per minute batch medians 8.46 to 9.7. Diagram and build shots run about 10 to 18 s (bVjKL1zd-vQ s028 15.8 s, v27fd0fa271 s047 18.2 s, uBwkMc1llmM s014 17.8 s). Over the whole corpus the code finds the longest still stretch per film at median 5 s and maximum 24 s, with 10 of 96 films holding over 10 s; the batch figures for longest hold were computed on truncated input and are superseded by these.
- **The previous subject morphs into the next subject** `outline_morph_join` [transition, 45 videos, Loom: needs_new_primitive]: One isolated shape carries the idea into the next shot: its outline and fill interpolate into the next subject (a circle into a hub node, a metronome into a heart, a seed into an eye, a crowd into one worker, a date label into a coin), so the join is a change rather than a cut and the change itself explains the link. When: Causal, combining or transformational ideas where a cut would break the link: one thing becoming another, two things merging, the next idea literally growing from the last. Timing: Morph motions median about 0.7 to 1.5 s across batches (p90 2.7 to 5.3 s); transform-into median 0.7 to 1.0 s.
- **Colour script: one dominant field per section** `section_colour_script` [colour, 40 videos, Loom: needs_param]: Each narrative section or chapter gets its own dominant field colour (and mood pair) while the accent rule, type, cards and drawing language stay constant, so the palette itself chapters the film without title cards. Some films darken the field step by step through a descent, or turn warm interiors cold at an emotional turn. When: Films with distinct chapters (problem, mechanism, limits, future), multi-stage processes, descents in depth or scale, and emotional turns. Timing: Sections in these records run roughly 10 to 70 s each (one colour script: 15 to 50 s per hue; another 22.6 to 73.6 s per segment); field swaps ride 0.6 to 1.0 s wipes.
- **A flat hand enters from the frame edge to act** `disembodied_hand_enters_to_act` [character, 39 videos, Loom: needs_assets]: A single simplified hand, with no arm or body, enters from the nearest frame edge on the action word, performs one action on the composition (place, present, pick, remove, tick, draw, turn a dial, stamp) and leaves the way it came, so the result holds alone. At large scale the same hand stands for an outside force: an institution, a policy or support that lifts, cradles or removes small figures. When: Any simple human action on a diagram or still life where a full character would distract: choosing one option, removing one unit, presenting evidence, ticking a checklist, building step by step, or showing a system acting on people. Timing: slide_in median 0.67 to 0.79 s and exit_slide median 0.79 to 0.83 s (code, per batch); a hand beat fills 2 to 13.6 s shots; a quick helper hand can enter and exit in under 0.5 s; a two-hand clasp eases out over about 4 s and holds.
- **Split screen or equal panels for comparison** `split_screen_comparison` [composition, 38 videos, Loom: needs_param]: Two, three or four equal panels divided by a thin seam, gutter or hard edge show the same subject at identical scale and orientation, so the one variable that differs is the only difference. Timing: character_act median 0.5 to 0.63 s (code); a whole property demonstration runs within a 10.6 s shot; single emotional beats 2.4 to 3.4 s.
- **Diagram assembled part by part as the parts are named** `diagram_assembled_in_named_order` [sync, 11 videos, Loom: exists]: A mechanism or system diagram is built on one held frame in the order the narration names its parts, starting from the bare central object, so the viewer builds the machine in step with the voice; connecting lines are drawn only after both of their ends exist. When: Mechanisms, procedures and system diagrams. Timing: Builds run about 10 to 16 s on one held frame (QyRqlTV60zM s073 14.8 s, ul-c6a4QkiU s020b 15.9 s); pop-in median 0.5 s, draw-on median 0.67 s per part.

## Rules
- [colour, always] Keep one saturated accent hue in the frame and put it on the element the narration is naming; everything else stays muted, desaturated or in one cool family. (check: Per frame outside the brand card and credits: cluster pixels above a high-chroma threshold by hue; pass at one accent cluster (more only when each extra hue is a declared code or comparison side) whose box overlaps the element named in the current beat's word timings. - [typography, usually] A label sits on the thing it names, arrives on its word and clears before the next label or idea; labels never pile up into a legend, and keys and leader lines are avoided. - [character, usually] Show death, injury, violence and destruction by symbol: one snappy motion plus a flat colour mark, a small burst, X eyes, a flash or a fade of light, then a hold; no anatomical detail or animated wounds. (check: Judgement; the state change completes in under 1 s on its keyword.)
- [camera, usually] Lock the camera by default; meaning comes from change inside the frame, in the drawing (morph, transform, draw-on, redraw), not from moving the whole picture. (check: Share of shots with no camera move 0.76 to 0.86 per batch (607 of 799 to 815 of 945); flag a lesson below about 0.7. - [camera, usually] When the camera moves, it makes one slow move on one axis (push, pull, pan, tilt or truck) that eases and settles; no handheld, shake, whip or compound moves, and a shake is kept for a single shock. (check: Per shot: at most one move segment, one dominant axis, no direction reversal or velocity spike; compound 'multiple' camera tags in only 2 to 6 shots per batch; shake in at most one shot per film.)
- [camera, usually] Use a push-in to isolate or emphasise a named subject and a pull-out to reveal scale or context; pans, tilts and follows are rarer and kept for reading a row or a reveal. (check: Push-in and pull-out are the leading moves in most batches (push_in 31 to 57 and pull_out 12 to 62 shots per batch; which one leads varies, and batch 06 also has 46 right pans); each move is tagged reveal or focus in the storyboard.)
- [camera, usually] Tie each move to the narration: a push starts on the noun that names its target and holds once framed; a reveal move ends on the word it reveals. (check: Timeline against Loom word timings: push start or reveal end inside the keyword's interval, followed by a static hold. In the films this timing is the vision model's description, not a measured offset.)
- [transition, usually] The hard cut is the default join, on the narration beat. (check: Hard cuts are 0.63 to 0.79 of joins per batch and 0.53 to 0.93 per film in batch 08. These counts include boundaries between sub-shots of one long shot, which are not real joins, so treat them as approximate.)
- [transition, usually] Every gradual join carries a meaning tied to the content: morph for becoming or idea to idea, zoom-through for entering a place, layer or scale, dissolve or redraw for passing time or emotion, a shaped wipe or iris for a chapter, topic reset or caused change, a pan for travelling along a list or between scales. Gradual joins sit at section, world or scale boundaries; inside a scene the beats are hard cuts. (check: Each non-cut join carries a meaning tag in the storyboard and coincides with a section, world, time or scale change; joins at segment boundaries are gradual more often than joins inside segments.)
- [transition, usually] Build a gradual join out of what is in the frame: the outgoing subject becomes the next (morph, object transform), a departing object, an opening or a portal carries the eye, or the wipe is cut from the subject's own material. Thematic wipes are fast. (check: Judgement at each non-cut join: name the element that persists or drives the change, and fail when none is named; the same shape and colour in the last frame of shot n and the first frame of shot n+1; wipe_in median 0.63 s (batch 03).)
- [transition, usually] Plain dissolves and fades through black are rare: a dissolve marks time, links a person to a place or releases emotion, and a fade through black is one quiet breath before a final or darker turn, about once per film at most. Some of the films' dissolves hold a deliberate ghost of the outgoing shot; Loom's double-exposure check at joins would flag that, so it is a gate conflict to resolve, not a defect of the films. (check: Dissolves 4 to 26 joins per batch of 529 to 1068 (under 3 percent); fade_through_black 1 to 4 per batch; flag more than 2 fades per lesson (batch 03).)
- [transition, usually] When the subject stays the same, change the picture in place (redraw, swap, add, or hold and change on the same ground) instead of cutting to a new scene. (check: Judgement on consecutive beats about the same subject. The records' none_continuous join counts cannot support this rule numerically, because many of them fall on boundaries between sub-shots of one long shot.)
- [pacing, usually] Pick a tempo family and hold it through the film: slow diagram films cut about 5 to 6 times a minute, fast story films 12 to 15 or more, and most films sit near 8 to 13. (check: Code cuts per minute per film: batch medians 8.3 to 15.0; per-film p10 to p90 about 4 to 20 across batches. Sub-second bursts stay short and are followed by a longer shot. - [pacing, usually] Keep something meaningful moving in most seconds, even with a locked camera. ## Avoid
- Moving the camera for energy or for its own sake: handheld shake, whip pans, orbits, drift, compound moves in one shot, pans as connective motion, or tracking a subject to show speed.: The camera is locked in about three quarters or more of shots and moves once, slowly, to reveal, focus or change scale; a moving lens on moving content loses the reading frame. Speed is shown by moving the background behind a fixed subject.
- Two or more unrelated saturated accents competing in one frame.: The single accent on the named element is the main focal cue in every batch; a second accent splits the eye and makes the frame read like a slide or a poster. Several hues appear only as a coded set, the two sides of a comparison, a title or end tableau, or a deliberate abundance or chaos beat.
- Lip-synced dialogue and detailed or realistic facial acting on drawn characters.: An off-screen narrator does the talking and posture carries emotion; lip sync appears in only a handful of shots per batch, nearly all live action, and detailed faces compete with the accent and break the minimal design.
- Bullet lists, sentences, paragraphs or caption strips that restate the narration.: Text is a label, term or number and the picture does the explaining; lists become pictures that build one item per spoken item, and text that must be read in full lives on an artefact in the world. List text appears in only a few shots per batch.
- Axis charts, floating number captions or bare abstract diagrams as the main carrier of a number or a mechanism.: Numbers ride objects (tags, counters, units, queues) and get a picture anchor when spoken; data-visualisation shots are 2 to 5 per batch, and even data beats carry an enacting figure, object or place.
- Text that cannot be read: loose over a busy or textured scene, over a face, under about 0.03 of frame height, or in a decorative script the audience cannot read.: Labels sit on objects, tags or backings beside the subject; text under 0.03 of frame height is UI texture, signage or credits only, and a write-on device still needs legible type.
- Crossfades, plain dissolves or fades through black as the routine join between scenes.: Hard cuts carry the rhythm and gradual joins carry a meaning; dissolves are under 3 percent of joins and fades through black one to four per batch. The few deliberate ghost-hold dissolves would trip Loom's double-exposure check at joins, which is a gate conflict to resolve, not a licence to use more of them.
- Generic or one-off effect transitions (spins, flips, star wipes) with no idea in them, or outside the film's own small set of joins.: Joins are made from material in the frame and each film keeps a small join grammar; a stray effect reads as a template trick.
- A frozen frame with nothing moving and nothing new to read while the voice runs on.: Motion share is high in every batch, and holds are spent on a key line, a definition, a diagram or the end card, usually with an ambient layer. The code over all holds puts the longest still per film at a median of 5 s, but 10 of 96 films hold over 10 s (up to 24 s), so length alone does not make a hold a defect; Loom's static-hold check has to allow a deliberate one.
- Pictures, labels or changes that ignore the sentence being spoken, arrive after their word has passed, or linger after the idea.: Word-bound change is the spine of these films; the vision model labels only a few percent of shots unrelated (mostly breathers, transitions and end cards) and almost none lagging. This is the model's description, not a measured offset.
- Mixing registers or finishes without a shared treatment: raw photos or footage, 3D, gloss or gradient-shaded figures as the base layer, a texture or ground change between cuts, or a hard cut into a new register with nothing carried across.: One finish and flat shading hold the world together; photo, footage and 3D appear as brief inserts that are framed, tinted or graded and bracketed by a marked join, otherwise they read as pasted in.
- Visual vocabulary that does not belong: stock clip-art icons, a concept redrawn with a new icon or colour between shots, or a new character for a state change of the same person.: Every icon is drawn in the film's own hand; one token design and fixed colour meanings let the viewer track an idea across worlds, and the same figure morphs or swaps pose so identity carries.
- Individually animated crowds, long full-body animation, or walk cycles as the main source of motion.: Crowds are repeated glyphs and acting is short gestures (character_act median about 0.5 to 1 s); walk cycles are about 17 to 20 events per batch, mostly for journeys and chases, because walking costs time and shows nothing.
- Graphic or anatomical depiction of death, injury or violence.: Harm is symbolic: one snappy motion, a flat red mark, a burst, X eyes, a flash or a fade; this stays readable and suits wide and employee audiences.
- Juvenile, gross-out or horror set pieces as the payoff of a beat, and real trademarks, song lyrics or attributed quotes reproduced on screen.: The records' own notes flag these gags as wrong for adults at work even where the films use them: keep the device and drop the gag. Trademarks, lyrics and quotes are content, not technique, and cannot be reused in client work.
- Building in the wrong order: a finished diagram with every label at once, two ideas animating together, labels piling up into a legend or a key with leader lines, or a scene that starts from an empty frame and assembles everything on screen.: Parts arrive on their words in staggered waves onto a composed scene, labels sit on what they name and clear after their clause, and one idea animates at a time. Loom's empty-frame check agrees about empty starts, but it would also flag the deliberate near-empty rest beats some films use.
- Crowded frames with little negative space, and detailed, outlined or high-contrast backgrounds competing with figures or sitting behind diagrams.: Negative space is medium or high in most shots; when density is needed it is contained in a frame or grid, or used on purpose to show a crowd problem, and backgrounds stay flat, low in contrast or single-hue so the subject reads first.
- Starting the narration on frame one or over the opening card.: Nearly every film keeps a silent opening card and starts speaking at about 6 to 8.7 s; the exceptions are live-host or hybrid films.
- Lower-third name bars, and identity labels that stay on screen through the content.: People are named by a badge, nameplate or hand-drawn banner in the art on the spoken name, and it leaves before the speaker's point.
- Emphasis devices used continuously: constant particles and sparkle, or sub-second cutting sustained through a whole film.: Particles and fast bursts are punctuation at peaks; most films keep a per-film median shot of 2.8 to 4.6 s (batch 08), and one film at 1.4 s shots and 30.67 cuts per minute is the outlier.

## Judge rubric
- **Composition and focus** (composition, weight 0.12): 1 = Several elements of equal size, brightness and colour scattered edge to edge; no subject; or a slide layout (title on top, a block of text or icons below); text overlaps the subject. / 3 = A subject can be found, but a second bright element or busy background competes; everything centred by habit with even margins; empty areas look left over rather than designed. / 5 = One subject, centred, on a third or on a strong diagonal, carrying the only saturated accent; about four to six elements; a framing device (light pool, lens, window, monitor, panel seam) leads the eye; dense material sits inside a container or grid; a deliberately empty rest frame or a wide field around a tiny subject counts as composed, not empty.
- **Legibility** (legibility, weight 0.1): 1 = Labels under about 0.03 of frame height, thin type on busy texture, sentences or bullet lists on screen, text over faces, or labels that flash off before they can be read. / 3 = Readable, but sizes are uniform (numbers no bigger than labels), contrast is weak on some fields, fonts are mixed or generic, or text lingers after its clause. / 5 = One to three words per text block; labels about 0.05 of frame height sitting on or pointing to what they name; titles about 0.12 or larger; key numbers about 0.12 to 0.18, larger than labels and alone; high contrast on a quiet area; lettering consistent with the style (hand, serif, monospace or clean sans all qualify); text clears when its job is done.
- **Visual explanation** (visual_explanation, weight 0.16): 1 = Generic icons or stock-like scenes beside the words, or text cards restating the narration; the frame would fit any script; numbers only as captions. / 3 = Literal illustration of the nouns spoken; a relevant diagram exists but sits still while the voice explains the cause, change or difference. / 5 = A visible device for each claim: a cutaway or cross-section where one part changes on its word, a count as repeated objects with an accented subset, a number written on a familiar object, a pull-back from one unit to the crowd, matched before and after on identical geometry, a lens or zoom-through to the hidden scale, a wrong model struck or morphed into the right one in place.
- **Motion with purpose** (motion_purpose, weight 0.1): 1 = Everything bounces, floats or spins at once; robotic linear slides; the camera drifts for no reason; motion unrelated to the words. / 3 = Tidy but uniform: every element enters the same way; the meaningful change is not clearly the main event; some decorative motion competes with the subject. / 5 = One main event per beat (a draw-on, a morph, a stamp, a gesture, an impact then hold); short eased entrances (pop about 0.4 to 0.6 s, slide under 1 s), transformations about 0.7 to 1.2 s then a hold; exits quicker than entrances; one slow ambient loop keeps a hold alive; a still subject while the world moves is a valid choice; a deliberate rest beat with almost nothing moving is pacing, not a defect.
- **Continuity of joins** (continuity, weight 0.1): 1 = Arbitrary effect wipes or spins between unrelated frames; palette, scale or drawing style jumps with no reason; a subject jumps position between related beats; visible seams or elements popping out mid-move. / 3 = Clean hard cuts and a consistent look, but nothing carries across; every gradual join is the same generic transition whatever the change. / 5 = Hard cuts on phrase boundaries inside an idea; at a change of idea the outgoing subject becomes the next (a morph, a carried object, a push through an opening, a wipe made of the scene's own material); a deliberate dissolve or brief ghost hold marks time, memory or a gentle cut and is judged as intended; one ground, motif or token returns across distant scenes; the ending can call back to the opening.
- **Style consistency** (style_consistency, weight 0.1): 1 = Mixed icon families, line weights or shading models with no reason; clean stock vector pasted into a drawn world; inserts that look pasted in; colours that change meaning; looks like a template whatever the genre. / 3 = One recognisable style with lapses: an off-model prop, a scene that drops the outline or texture rule, a second accent creeping in, or details that look taken from a kit. / 5 = One outline rule, one fill and shading model, one finish and one lettering hand across every shot, diagrams and cards included; one accent with one meaning; a deliberate register break (a dark void, a neon interlude, a live insert, a studio outro) is short, bracketed by a clear join and consistent within itself, and counts as design, not a style mix.
- **Pacing** (pacing, weight 0.08): 1 = Every shot the same length; or stretches over about 8 s where nothing changes and nothing is meant to be read; or relentless sub-second cutting that leaves labels unread. / 3 = A reasonable average (shots around 3 to 5 s) but flat: definitions, lists and emotional beats get the same time. / 5 = A chosen tempo held within the film (slow paper-diagram films and fast story films both qualify); 1 to 3 s beats through lists and montage peaks; 5 to 15 s builds where something is added every few seconds; a held key line or a wordless release beat after a dense idea; a calm open and an ending that slows into the close.
- **Polish and finish** (polish, weight 0.1): 1 = Looks like a slide or an amateur template: default fonts and shapes, aliased or uneven strokes, misaligned elements, placeholder text, half-built frames, jitter. / 3 = Clean and tidy but plain: sterile vector where the style wants surface, or a texture that looks like a filter laid on top; generic easing; a thrown-together end card. / 5 = Every frame worth pausing on: crafted custom shapes, a deliberate finish that suits the style and survives compression (paper, grain, halftone, glow, or a clean flat surface for a clean style), soft vignettes or light where used, small lived-in details, composed from the first frame of each shot, a tidy designed close.
- **Character** (character, weight 0.06): 1 = Stiff stock or bean figures, inconsistent designs between shots, detailed but lifeless faces, constant fidgeting, or babyish mascots and gross-out gags in an adult lesson. / 3 = Consistent simple figures who mostly stand beside the idea; poses do not change with the narration; crowds drawn as many individuals acting at once. / 5 = Few figures per shot, one design grammar for the cast; emotion carried by posture, one feature or one gesture that peaks on the keyword; a persistent agent tracked in one colour; crowds as repeated figures or silhouettes around one active subject; a hand that enters to act and leaves; neutral, professional tone. Frames with no character score on the other questions, not here.
- **Sync with narration** (sync, weight 0.08): 1 = Elements appear seconds before they are named or long after; whole lists appear at once; counters stop on a different number than the one spoken; overlays linger into the next idea. / 3 = Shots change on sentence boundaries, but events inside the shot are loosely timed or arrive a clause early. / 5 = Each pop, label, morph, gesture and stamp completes on its naming or result word; lists build one item per spoken item; counters settle on the spoken figure; cuts sit on phrase boundaries; overlays clear before the next idea.

## Open questions
- Sync lead: sync_offset_s is a value the vision model wrote (0.0 for 6,116 of 6,307 shots), not a measurement, and frames were sampled near 1 fps. Do entrances really lead the word by 0.2 to 0.5 s, as many records describe? Measure against word timings on full-frame-rate video before any sync gate uses a number.
- Morph or dissolve: how many of the morphs and object-transform joins (the batch records report morphs in at least 48 of the 60 films of batches 01, 03, 05, 07 and 08) are true outline interpolations rather than dissolves or fast cuts sampled at 1 fps? This decides the priority of a path-morph primitive.
- Grain and paper strength: how much of the texture is artwork and how much is 480p compression? Check a few films at full resolution before moving Loom's finish default (grain near off, D-100) toward visible strength.
- One accent or one dominant accent: every record claims one saturated accent per frame, yet several shots describe two. A per-frame chroma cluster count with hex values is needed before an accent gate can be calibrated.
- Line boil: is the hand-line flicker in the sketch and wobbly families real frame-to-frame boil or compression shimmer? If it is static wobble, the existing seeded rough.js sketch mode may be enough.
- Pacing on redraw films: the cut detector misses redraw-in-place changes (jLOuMXnM5wk has 0 detected cuts; several films are split into equal sub-shot windows), and shot-list boundaries and detected joins disagree both ways. Which source should pacing rules use, and what are the true longest holds per film?
- Live and talk formats: should the 10 live or stage films (live_action_hybrid) count toward house constants and pacing targets, or be a separate format? They break the silent open, the single narrator and the pacing ranges.
- Gate conflicts: Loom's double-exposure warning (loom/qa.py) exempts only 'continue' joins on one station, so it would flag deliberate ghost-hold dissolves; the empty-frame check (loom/checks/film.py) errors unless the beat intent is hold, sparse or dark. Should a dissolve or ghost hold get its own declared intent, and should rest beats be declared by default in packs that use them?
- Judge calibration: the same vision model condemned 35 % of clean windows of real films (mostly crossfades, style mixes, motion jank and static holds). Does this style-aware rubric, with deliberate dissolves and rest beats written into the 5 anchors, cut those false condemnations without losing recall on planted defects? Re-run the 493 clean windows and the planted set.
- Sound: no sound records were made. What does the 4.3 to 7.9 dB 'bed below speech' figure measure, and which visual events carry the sparse non-speech hits (about 5 per minute)? No sound rule should be built until a sound pass runs.

## Full technique library (all 175 merged techniques; the 60 above are a breadth-first selection)

### camera
- **Locked camera by default; one move with one meaning when it moves** `static_camera_default` [60 films, Loom: exists]: The frame is locked in most shots and the motion budget goes to the content: morphs, stamps, draw-ons, particles, gestures and ambient loops supply all the movement, and rhythm comes from cuts and shot-size changes. When the camera does move, it is one slow move with one meaning (push in to emphasise or enter, pull out to reveal scale, tilt to reveal a destination, truck to follow a route), never coverage and never compound.
- **Pull back from one detail to reveal scale or context** `pull_back_scale_reveal` [54 films, Loom: exists]: One continuous pull-out starts tight on a highlighted unit or ambiguous detail (a tile, a rack, a figure, a child on a rope, a flat bar) and reveals it as one of many, or as the surface of something larger (a fleet, a lattice, a crowd, a city, a globe); the subjects hold still so the wide reads as a reframe of the same world, not a new scene, often with a tiny human for scale.
- **Slow push-in that lands on the named subject** `push_in_on_named_subject` [48 films, Loom: exists]: A slow push-in over a held picture isolates one subject from a busy field, tightens on a face or symbol, and arrives as the key word or name is spoken, adding weight without adding information; a label may land once the object fills the frame, a very slow push on a nearly still figure builds unease or inward focus, and a sub-second push makes a reaction punch.
- **Slow lateral pan along a row, each item entering on its name** `pan_along_row_on_spoken_list` [31 films, Loom: exists]: One slow horizontal pan or truck walks the eye along a row, wall, shelf, lineup, trail or long structure wider than the frame, so each item enters as the narrator names it and many reads without a cut per item; labels or data marks pop as items enter, and the move ends on the item that matters, often a contrasting or accent-coloured payoff.
- **Slow tilt up to a light or down to a hidden level** `tilt_reveal` [13 films, Loom: exists]: One continuous slow tilt carries the eye vertically: down from sky or canopy to ground or underground to discover a hidden level, or up from a grounded subject (onlookers, a pile of the fallen) through parallax layers to a skyline or one bright source for a hopeful or epic turn.
- **Overhead flat-lay or top-down drift** `overhead_view` [7 films, Loom: exists]: A top-down view flattens a table, floor, room, garden or diorama into a diagram-like layout: as a short insert isolating one hand action or a symmetric arrangement, or as a slow drift over many repeated small figures and props to show scale or a whole environment at once.
- **Slow pan or parallax drift over a still illustration** `parallax_drift_over_still` [7 films, Loom: exists]: A static, wide illustration comes alive through one slow pan, truck or parallax drift across two or three depth layers while the figures stay still; any label stays fixed while the background moves beneath it.
- **Cut to a close insert on the key gesture** `cut_to_insert_on_gesture` [6 films, Loom: exists]: From a wide establishing frame the film hard-cuts to a close insert exactly when the key gesture or object matters (a card handed over, a blocked passenger, an unfinished line on paper, a laptop screen), holds it only while the object is named, then returns.
- **Camera arcs up and over to an overhead view** `camera_arc_to_overhead` [2 films, Loom: needs_new_primitive]: The camera swings around and up from eye level beside the subject to an overhead view in one continuous move, revealing the shape of a journey or a city.

- **Threat larger and angular, protagonist small and round** `threat_larger_angular` [4 films, Loom: needs_assets]: Threats and pressures are drawn at least twice the protagonist's height, angular or spiky, or as dark silhouettes with one accent; the protagonist is small and round, so size and shape carry the power relation before any action.
- **Live speaker annotated with flat graphics** `live_action_annotated_flat` [2 films, Loom: needs_assets]: Talking-head footage is graded or duotone-tinted toward the flat palette, held as a locked centred medium shot, and annotated by flat badges, keywords and small drawn stand-ins that pop on the spoken term, between short flat cutaways.

### colour
- **One saturated accent on the narrated element** `single_accent_on_muted_field` [96 films, Loom: needs_param]: The frame sits in one muted, desaturated or single-hue family and exactly one element, the one the narration names in the current clause, carries a saturated hue. When the narration moves to a new subject the accent moves with it and the previous element is demoted back to the field family.
- **Colour script: one dominant field per section** `section_colour_script` [40 films, Loom: needs_param]: Each narrative section or chapter gets its own dominant field colour (and mood pair) while the accent rule, type, cards and drawing language stay constant, so the palette itself chapters the film without title cards. - **Near-black mode for the inner, microscopic or unknown** `dark_void_mode` [17 films, Loom: needs_param]: Abstract, microscopic, inner-mind or unknown beats switch the film into a near-black or navy void with one light line colour or one glowing subject and sparse drifting specks, then return to the lit world. A variant fills a character's head silhouette with the void and pins colour-blocked regions or a glowing core inside it.
- **One colour bound to one meaning for the whole film** `semantic_colour_map` [13 films, Loom: needs_param]: Beyond the focal accent, each recurring concept, entity or side of a polarity keeps one fixed hue for the whole film (red for the threat, gold only on authority, oxygen blue and hydrogen pink, warm versus cool poles, one costume accent per recurring speaker), so the colour becomes a label and diagrams read without a legend.

- **State change as a colour swap on a held object or layout** `colour_swap_as_state` [8 films, Loom: exists]: The layout never redraws; the current step or state is shown by changing the fill of the object itself: an active button turns green, pipes flood red, a ring of a plan turns red, an object desaturates and sinks to go dormant or saturates and rises to wake, a status light turns green to red before the event.
- **A new flat field colour for each new idea** `field_colour_per_idea` [6 films, Loom: needs_param]: Each short idea sits on its own single flat saturated field with no environment (lavender, yellow, red, green; pink stage, green pitch, cream oven), so a new idea is signalled by a new field while the drawing language and accent stay constant. - **Whole film inside one hue family** `monochrome_family_world` [5 films, Loom: needs_param]: The film stays in one hue family (pink, red-pink, black and white, paper and ink), varying only value and saturation per section, so even hard cuts feel like one world and the single off-family accent carries all emphasis.
- **Glow halo instead of an outline** `glow_halo_separation` [5 films, Loom: exists]: Flat, unstroked shapes are separated from a saturated or dark field by a soft halo or a thin light ring rather than a dark outline; a stronger glow marks the active element.
- **Cool actor inside a saturated warm interior world** `cool_actor_on_warm_interior` [4 films, Loom: needs_assets]: Inside-the-body or micro scenes flood the field with a saturated red, pink or warm monochrome with thin branching contour lines, and the active agent is the only cool blue or cyan element; passive elements stay white or pale. The film re-enters this same interior palette each time the process continues.
- **Grey live footage, saturated flat overlays** `desaturated_footage_saturated_overlay` [4 films, Loom: needs_assets]: Live interview footage is graded toward grey and all saturated colour belongs to flat cartoon overlays popped beside the face, so the graphics read as the idea and the person as the voice.
- **Still subject, cycling sky or season palette** `palette_cycle_time_lapse` [3 films, Loom: needs_param]: The subject holds still while the background palette cycles day to night or season to season, often with a sun and moon moving, compressing time into a few seconds.
- **Same silhouette, new colours for each variant** `recolour_variant_series` [1 films, Loom: exists]: A series of variants or types is shown by hard-cutting the same silhouette with a new body palette and a swapped field colour while pose and head position stay fixed, so variety reads with no new drawings.

## Explanation patterns
- **comparison (two conditions, two outcomes, variation or universality)** (29 films): equal split panels or mirrored subjects that vary one element. Divide the frame with a thin gutter or hard edge, or stand both items on one shared baseline, and place the same subject at the same scale and pose on each side; colour-code the sides warm and cool. Establish the pictures first, then change only the variable under test and pop each side's number or effect icon on its spoken value, with labels last. Resolve with one gesture: slide the loser away, strike the rejected cause, or tip a balance beam. For 'it varies', demote the hero to one tile among variants; for 'it is universal', play one gesture in sync across differently coloured cells. A single object morphing in place under a fixed term band is the one-frame variant.
- **quantity (a large count, volume or 'many')** (27 films): repeated identical units that accumulate. Design one small unit that reads at tiny size and show it alone first. Stagger identical copies in, timed to the spoken count, into a row, grid, ring, stack or onto a map or silhouette until the set reads as many; switch to smaller markers when it gets dense. Hold the full set still, or pull back to show its extent, or drift the camera along a row so more is implied than shown. Reorganise or remove units to show change, or cross-dissolve the same units into a familiar place so the number becomes concrete. Use units instead of an on-screen figure unless the figure must be stated.
- **structure (what is inside, its parts and layers)** (27 films): cutaway, unfold or magnifier with each part named on its word. Show the closed whole first. Open it on the naming word: split it into a cross-section, unfold it, turn it translucent, explode it along one axis, or tie a circular lens to the exact spot with a leader line. Reduce the body or object to a flat silhouette container, fill the named region with the accent and pop a short label with at most one leader line exactly on the spoken name; for stacks, add one layer per named part over a constant reference line and keep a small figure for scale. Cross-fade lens content per sub-idea, close the lens or swap back to the whole when the sentence ends.
- **scale (one part of a much larger or much smaller system)** (21 films): one continuous camera move between levels. To go up a level, start on one hero object or a private detail and pull back in one continuous move until it is one of many identical copies, a tile in a lattice, a speck in a vast field or a ring of figures on a globe; flatten the receding copies to silhouettes and keep a small human for scale. To go down a level, push through an opening in the familiar surface (a cell, a screen, a skin) into a darker micro field, carry the accent colour through, let specks resolve into agents, type the term on as the scale lands, then pull back to the whole system.
- **spread, route or journey across a territory or system** (19 films): a muted map as a stage with one accent path and one marker per place. Flatten the map or system to two muted tones with thin labels and hold it still. Pop one uniform marker per spoken place in the warm accent, staggered in the direction of spread, and stamp the year when it matters; or grow one accent ribbon, dashed line or moving dot along the route in travel order, landing the arrowhead on the destination as it is named. Mark stopped advances with X stamps, grow a region in small steps per clause, and keep a small corner locator when the path goes deep inside a system.
- **quantity (one headline statistic or an extreme magnitude)** (17 films): the number set on a physical object, a counter that locks, or a swap to a familiar object. Clear to one field and set the number large on or beside an object from the story (a sign, a bag, a container, a stopwatch). Roll a counter and lock it on the spoken value, filling a container or scaling the object in steps as it climbs; pop any emphasis burst only after the lock. Nest a subset inside the numeral or add one small figure enacting the measured behaviour. For extreme magnitudes, cross-dissolve the subject into a familiar object on the comparison word and hand-write the multiplier beside it, then dissolve back. Every figure comes from the source.
- **history (a sequence of eras, owners or versions)** (17 films): a constant anchor while the era swaps around it. Hold the character or key object in the same pose and position and cross-fade only the background props and room, or swap one attribute (a flag's colours, hair) per spoken era, or swap the old version into exactly the same position with callouts unmoved. Alternatively lay the eras as vignettes or framed panels along one wide strip and reveal them with one continuous pan, popping the year in a reserved colour on its mention and introducing people with a typed nameplate. Shift the palette to an era colour and keep a fixed year tag.
- **feeling or inner state (as a sign on or over the person)** (15 films): externalised symbol, glowing marks or a glowing double. Keep the figure still, reduced and in a matching posture, or a featureless dark silhouette on a saturated field. Grow one accent symbol out of or over it in stages timed to the words (a flame from the head, a watching eye, a lit chest, a scribble knot, a burst at the pain site), or pop small bright marks inside it; for a real speaker, wrap a wobbly glowing outline round them. An inner voice or ideal can be a soft glowing silhouette beside the crisp person that changes shape for each turn. Let the pose do the rest and clear the symbol when the state resolves.
- **process (an invisible actor doing several steps)** (13 films): personification: one small character, or one worker per step. Make the invisible actor one small character with a fixed silhouette, colour, face grammar and one prop, name it once with a written label, and let it physically perform every step (carry, read, tick, squeeze, operate) while bystanders react, so the cause is read from their faces; it stays the same character for the whole film. When there are several actors, split the narration into clauses and cast one small figure per clause with one job prop, animating only that job on a central object that transforms in place. An administrative procedure can be the same: one figure, one recurring prop, one physical action per spoken step.
- **feeling or power (as space and scale around the person)** (13 films): a small figure in a vast or dominating field. Put one small faceless figure low in a large dark, empty or flat field with a single soft glow or slow falling particles, and hold the camera still so only the atmosphere moves; let weather or closing frames track the intensity. For pressure or power, let a large figure, force or hand fill the rest of the frame with rim light on its silhouette, or a soft shadow loom beside the person.
- **misconception or claim (being judged, revised or corrected)** (12 films): state the claim, hold it, then negate or correct it in place. Write or stage the claim or myth literally and hold it long enough to be believed. On the word that introduces the problem, draw a red strike-through, a scribble or a bold X over it, or slam a tilted stamp or prohibition symbol in the accent only, keeping the original visible; swap the corrected word into the same line, or cut to the evidence. When the claim is a model, keep its labels attached and morph its structure into the correct one in a single move, then hold the result as fact.
- **duration, frequency or a deadline** (12 films): a still subject under a changing cycle. Lock the subject and change only one cycling element: the sky's palette with a moving sun or moon, a water level rising and falling, ticks crossing off a calendar, a window turning day to night, or a clock whose hands turn with the narration. Let the viewer count repetitions instead of reading a number, keep one tiny secondary motion alive, and mark a deadline by freezing every figure on the strike. For a span, draw an arrow on under the subject in sync with the spoken length and land the number at its tip.
- **quantity (a share, prevalence or one case among many)** (11 films): uniform grid of identical figures with an accented subset, then one singled out. Lay out identical figures or units evenly on a flat field. On the word, tint, cross, wipe or slice the affected share in the accent, or change its faces or state marks with a stagger; stamp the percentage or a word across an empty band, or show no number at all. To move from group to case, push the camera into one unit until it fills the frame, or flag it with the accent and a short halo, and hold it as the new subject.
- **process (a change of state over time in one subject)** (11 films): draw it once, then change it in place. Draw the scene or the subject once, centred in a fixed set, and never redraw it. Show each stage as an in-place change tied to its spoken word: a colour wash creeping over the affected area, a fill or colour swap on a fixed cutaway, one dark shape growing over a long hold, accent particles bunching where a channel narrows, or the subject morphing through named states (ingredient to product, healthy to failed). For a collapse, hold the intact object about two seconds, then snap it to the new state so it completes on the spoken result. Instant swaps (X-eyes, a badge's text) suit sensitive outcomes. Hold the end state.
- **list (named items, roles, causes or qualities)** (11 films): one item per spoken word, in one place. Lay out empty slots or the diagram the items belong to, then fill them one per spoken name: icons of one family pop in a row with a one-word label, regions on a diagram light with a colour-matched bullet, or the glyph inside one fixed central container swaps per keyword. Keep the accent on the current item, truck slowly along long rows, end with a highlight on the key item, and clear the set before showing the exception.
- **invisible force, signal or measurement** (9 films): the force as the only saturated element: rings, a glowing line or a light beam. Switch to a plain or near-black field and draw the machine or scene flat or as wireframe. Reserve one accent hue for the signal and make it the only saturated thing: thin rings that pulse from the source, a glowing line that traces the flow, or a wedge or beam of light that sweeps a dark mass. Reveal, recolour or warm each object only when the signal reaches it, so the change has a visible cause; let findings accumulate as dots before any warning stamps. Collapse or flatten the rings to reset.
- **relationships and networks (roles, stakeholders, loops)** (9 films): node-and-line network built hub first. Fill one small outlined shape to become the hub, then pop the surrounding nodes in a stagger on the spoken list, or reveal evenly spaced satellites with a pull-out from the hub. Draw the connecting lines only after every node is present; add faces or link symbols after the structure is built, and mark the decision-maker with the accent. For a reciprocal loop between two parties, draw the first arrow when the first action happens and the return arrow only after the response.
- **structure or making process (parts combined in order)** (8 films): build the whole left to right in the order it is named. On a paper sheet or flat field, draw or bring in each part or step only when it is named, left to right, each snapping in with a small settle; give each part type its own shape and reuse that shape family in every later diagram. Reserve the accent for the risky or decisive step, and bring the user or the finished product in last.
- **rule, policy or principle (an abstraction with no picture)** (8 films): a physical apparatus or one object doing one action. Make the rule a machine or object the viewer can watch work: a booth that slices value off, a drum that drops one ball, a scroll that unrolls, a figure that steps out of bounds on a bare stage with one ground line. Isolate it in a soft light pool or on a flat field, run exactly one action from start to finish on the narration and mark the result with the accent. For an analogy, align the metaphor and the real thing on one axis, show the principle on the metaphor, then dissolve through a shared outline into the real thing while the principle overlay stays.
- **definition (naming a term)** (7 films): the term isolated on a bare field, then the concrete thing appears. Isolate the term in large display type on one plain or saturated field, lettering it on as it is spoken; then let the concrete thing surface under it, or present the subject as a labelled exhibit on a plinth whose nameplate completes as the term is said. To generalise, morph one concrete example into a ring of small varied examples around the category word. Open into the scene once the term has landed.
