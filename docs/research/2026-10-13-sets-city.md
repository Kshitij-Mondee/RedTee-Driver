## 0. The scenes kept (D-156)

Lesson moments imagined first: a pedestrian steps off the kerb looking at a phone; a wheelchair user waits at a
dropped kerb; a commuter checks a map to find the way; walking home after a late shift; a commuter works on a
confidential document on the tram and the neighbour can read it; a confidential call on the tram; joining free
public Wi-Fi on the commute; offering a priority seat; someone follows an employee through the office door
(tailgating); a visitor at the intercom; a courier at the door; leaving the building alone after dark; working in a
cafe on its Wi-Fi; a laptop left on a cafe table; a client conversation overheard over coffee. `py -m loom sets
demand` (2026-10-15) showed `city/transit` named by two lessons (https-private, "in transit") and `city/street` by one
(gps-position): both ids are kept. The moments happen in four places of one district:

| scene | reuse | why it stays |
|---|---|---|
| `street` (the high street's signal crossing) | high | crossing, distraction, wayfinding, accessibility, the walk home |
| `transit` (inside the low-floor tram) | high | working and talking in public on the commute, Wi-Fi, priority seats |
| `office_exterior` (the mill's entrance) | medium | tailgating, visitors, couriers, leaving late |
| `cafe` (a cafe in an old shop unit) | medium | public Wi-Fi, shoulder surfing, unattended laptops, overheard talk |

Dropped: `plaza` (no lesson moment above happens in a square; a public square is a filler backdrop) and
`night_street` (it was the street at night: now the street's `night` variant). The office exterior is the same
converted 1920s mill the office world is set in (`packs/worlds/office.json`), so the two worlds join up.

The scene id `street` is also an everyday word: the gate refuses any other city scene that says "street", so the
other scenes say "road" or "high road". Plan checks: `docs/assets/plan_checks/city/<scene>.json` (all four).

## 1. The tram

| Fact used in the brief | Source |
|---|---|
| Low-floor tram widths 2.30 to 2.65 m (Avenio Munich 2.30 m, Avenio Bremen 2.65 m); a 2.4 m car has about 2.2 m inside | (s) https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:df7fd584-b98c-4d30-b183-730fa6408e6a/siemens-mobility-avenio-bremen-en.pdf ; https://www.solarisbus.com/en/press/pdf/344 |
| Double doors about 1.3 m clear; single doors 0.65 to 0.8 m | (s) the Avenio datasheets above |
| Floor height about 300 to 350 mm at the doors (Puma: 300 mm at the entrances, 360 in the vestibules; CAF Urbos 350 mm along the whole car) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HCP_Puma (read 2026-10-15); (s) https://www.caf.net/en/soluciones/familia/urbos/accesibilidad.php |
| Seats mostly 2+2; a wheelchair or pushchair space near a double door; heaters under seats near the doors | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HCP_Puma (read) |
| Aisle widths vary by design (490 mm in one, 750 mm minimum in another) | (s) maker datasheets via the search above |
| Tram stop platforms on one UK system: about 32 m long, 350 mm above rail, all more than 2 m wide | (s) https://croydon-tramlink.co.uk/info/infra/stops.shtml |

Not verified: a seat back height. The brief uses high backs to 1.35 m with a head roll, a design choice (many tram
seats are lower) made so that each seated head is seen against its own seat back rather than a window; the solid
pillar behind each seat pair is the same kind of choice. Seat pitch and the 0.55 m aisle are also design choices.

## 2. Streets, crossings and footways

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Blister tactile paving across the full width of the dropped kerb, never on the transition kerbs; 1.2 m deep at in-line crossings or 0.8 m (Edinburgh now uses 0.8 m everywhere, after UCL research found 0.8 m readily detectable) | (s) https://www.wexfordcoco.ie/sites/default/files/content/AccessForAll/WCC%20Accessibility%20Guidelines%20Edition%201%20%28June%202014%29.pdf ; https://edinburgh.gov.uk/downloads/file/24973/m4-tactile-paving |
| A dropped kerb is flush, at most 6 mm above the road | (s) the Wexford guidelines above |
| Signal crossings at least 2.4 m wide; the push button 1.0 to 1.1 m to its centre; the pole 0.35 to 0.5 m from the tactile paving's edge | (s) https://www.gloucestershire.gov.uk/media/gyko5u1f/gcc-standard-dwgs-2023-sd-009-tactiles-pole-setting-out.pdf ; https://consult.galway.ie/ga/system/files/materials/7677/3.%20CC-SCD-05137-01%20Tactile%20paving.pdf |
| Footways ideally 2 m clear (two wheelchairs pass), 1.5 m where that is not possible; full-height kerbs 75 to 100 mm | (s) https://www.eastlothian.gov.uk/info/210646/transport_infrastructure_in_new_developments/12631/transport_infrastructure_in_new_developments/11 ; https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/downloads/file/24977/p3---footways---version-11 ; https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1044542/inclusive-mobility-a-guide-to-best-practice-on-access-to-pedestrian-and-transport-infrastructure.pdf |
| Sheffield cycle stands about 0.75 m high and 0.75 m long (York); 0.8 m spacing in makers' and Bracknell Forest's drawings | (s) https://www.york.gov.uk/downloads/file/10394/city-centre-cycle-parking-strategy ; https://www.bracknell-forest.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2021-08/sheffield-cycle-standards.pdf |

Not verified: the colour of the blister paving is left to the style (councils differ: red at controlled crossings in
some, buff in others; red would pin a country, so the brief does not say red). The signal head's figures are drawn
plain (a red standing figure over a green walking one) without a country's exact pictogram. The 3.2 m crossing width
and the 7.3 m carriageway with two tram tracks about 3 m apart are design choices inside the ranges above.
