# Product explainer videos: what the guidance says (research behind skills/product/product-explainer)

STATUS: RESEARCH (2026-10-16). Web pages were read as data. Most sources are vendor or agency blogs selling video
work, so their figures are directional, not measured. Legal summaries come from law-firm commentary, not the primary
texts, and are not legal advice.

## Findings and where they went in the skill

1. **Length depends on placement.**
   - Homepage hero videos: about 60 to 90 s.
   - Paid social: 30 s or less.
   - Demos and sales follow-ups: longer, 2 to 5 min, depending on depth.
   - Sources conflict on demo length; the difference is overview versus walkthrough. Skill s2.
2. **Structure:** problem, impact, solution, proof, how it works, call to action; and PAS (problem, agitation,
   solution) variants. A five-part SaaS script runs problem, empathy, solution, proof, CTA. The solution is one
   "here's what changes" sentence, not a feature list. Skill s3; agitation kept to one beat.
3. **Benefit first, details later;** every claim has a visual counterpart on screen. Skill s4 (the "so that" ladder)
   and s5.
4. **One clear CTA,** softer for awareness, direct for decision-stage viewers. Skill s2 and s3.
5. **Positioning before messaging** (April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*, via secondary summaries): competitive
   alternatives (often a spreadsheet or doing nothing), unique attributes, value, target customers, market category.
   Skill s1, the truth sheet.
6. **Simplified UI (SUI):** blocks of colour standing for real UI, used for clarity with unfamiliar audiences. It is
   anonymised and paired with a heading. Brand guides warn that changes must only make the illustration easier to
   understand, and some brands avoid drawing their software in order to keep trust. Skill s5, fidelity ladder,
   faithful rule.
7. **Pointer:** highlight it, smooth its path, and never wiggle to point. Show the action and then the result.
   Skill s5.
8. **Sound off:** the first 3 s decide whether people stay and are often muted, so on-screen copy carries the arc.
   Skill s7.
9. **FTC Endorsement Guides** (revised 2023-06-29, per law-firm summaries):
   - endorsements must be truthful and substantiated;
   - material connections are disclosed clearly and conspicuously;
   - testimonials implying atypical results need the generally expected results disclosed;
   - in video, disclosures are audio and visual, close to the claim, possibly repeated, and a corner line may be
     missed;
   - no manipulation of reviews.

   Skill s6.
10. **FTC comparative advertising policy** (16 CFR 14.15): truthful comparisons are allowed and are judged by the
    same standard as other ads; the reasonable-basis factors come from the Pfizer decision. Practitioners advise
    head-to-head substantiation for superiority claims. Skill s6, which names the alternative category by default.

## Not verified

- The primary FTC texts (16 CFR Part 255) and the current status of the related reviews rule were not read directly.
- No platform's current safe-zone figures were checked; the skill tells the author to check them per platform.
