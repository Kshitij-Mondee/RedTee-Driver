# World packs: scenes, cast and the most common assets per setting

GENERATED by `py tools/world_packs.py` from `packs/worlds/*.json`. Edit the JSON, then regenerate.

**What a world pack is.** The setting a lesson lives in: its scenes (sets in depth layers), its cast, and the
assets that setting almost always needs. An office will always need desks, chairs, screens and mugs: the
`core` list is that, `common` is frequent, `optional` is occasional. Characters are briefed and reviewed with
`skills/lnd/lnd-character-design`; prompts are assembled from `docs/assets/CHARACTER_PROMPTS.md` and the
contract in `docs/ASSET_WISHLIST.md` (Prompt 0, Prompt 0+, Prompt R).

**Directed worlds (brief 2)** have one paste-ready prompt per scene and style in `docs/assets/world_prompts/`
(index: `docs/assets/world_prompts/README.md`), with real-world scale and object construction checked against
references (`docs/research/2026-10-09-set-references.md`). The other worlds still carry the short prompt below
until they are directed the same way.

**Styles are never compared with each other** (D-122). Each world lists the styles it is commonly made in;
the lesson chooses one, and a whole pack is drawn in that one style.

## City and public spaces (`city`)

**For lessons on:** the commute and travel to work, safety on foot and in public, working in public places, arriving at the office, accessibility in public, customer journeys.

**Commonly made in:** flat 2D editorial, isometric, 2.5D lit layers, glow-flat (not a ranking).

**The place:** The inner district of a mid-sized, temperate city along one tram line: a Victorian and Edwardian high street of three- and four-storey buildings with shops at street level and a civic library, and one block off it the converted 1920s brick textile mill that now holds offices. The tram runs along the high street on a pair of tracks set into the road. Busy but human in scale: no towers, no motorway, nothing newer than a few infill buildings of the 1990s.

**Scenes**

| id | what | shots |
|---|---|---|
| `street` | the high street at its signal-controlled crossing, seen from the near footway: the crossing runs away across the road and its two tram tracks to the far kerb, where the plain stone front of the old public library stands behind a wide footway, with shop fronts either side | wide, mid |
| `transit` | inside the city's low-floor tram, seen from the door area: two pairs of high-backed seats facing the camera either side of the aisle, big windows on both sides with the city passing, yellow grab poles and rails | wide, mid |
| `office_exterior` | the entrance of a converted 1920s brick textile mill, now an office building, seen from the footway: a glazed screen with sliding doors set into an old cart opening under a segmental brick arch, the plain brick base of the stair tower beside it with a card reader, a 20-pane steel window at the left | wide, mid |
| `cafe` | a long, narrow cafe in a Victorian shop unit on the high road, seen from a table near its front window: a row of small tables along an oxblood banquette on a deep bottle-green wall at the left, the counter with its coffee machine and pastry case down the right, a door to the back at the far end | wide, mid |

## History and period worlds (`history`)

**For lessons on:** how work used to be: the origins of safety law, pay and record keeping, fair trading and consumer protection, office work before computers, change over time.

**Commonly made in:** painterly, hand-drawn ink and wash, 2.5D lit layers, flat 2D editorial (not a ranking).

**The place:** One cotton-weaving town in a temperate valley, seen at two dates in the life of its main firm. The 1890s: the firm's north-light weaving shed of about 800 looms, its counting house in the stone office block beside the shed, and the town's market street on a market day. The 1950s: the same firm's general office, in the same stone block, sixty years on. Two dates are kept on purpose and no others: the 1890s carry the origins of factory safety law, wages and records, and fair trading; the 1950s carry how office work used to be done. Every scene says which date it is.

## Travel and hospitality (`travel`)

**For lessons on:** customer service for travel, booking and change policies, airport and hotel operations, duty of care, accessibility, fraud in bookings.

**Commonly made in:** flat 2D editorial, 2D story cartoon, isometric, 2.5D lit layers (not a ranking).

**The place:** A mid-sized international airport on the flat edge of a temperate coastal city, and the places a trip passes through around it, all built between 2014 and 2018 to one architect's design: the terminal (a 220 m long check-in hall and a pier of eight gates under one long roof of pale timber), a narrow-body jet at a gate, a 160-room hotel across the forecourt, and the contact centre of a mid-sized travel company on the second floor of an office building in the airport's business park, its windows looking out over the airfield.

**Scenes**

| id | what | shots |
|---|---|---|
| `airport_checkin` | the check-in hall of the terminal: one long island of check-in desks with weigh scales and bag belts in front of a deep teal spine wall, a two-lane queue of belt barriers to the left, flight information screens and a pictogram sign box hung overhead, under the hall's pale timber roof | wide, mid |
| `departure_gate` | a departure gate on the terminal's pier: a two-position gate podium in front of a deep teal gate wall, beam seating in the foreground, and the full-height airside glass on the left with the jet bridge door and a plain white jet's nose at the bridge, under a slatted pale timber ceiling | wide, mid |
| `aircraft_cabin` | the economy cabin of the plain white narrow-body jet seen from the forward entry area down its single aisle: rows of deep teal seats three and three, grey-blue overhead bins, small windows with their shades part down, and a plain rear bulkhead with a pale grey curtain at the far end | mid, wide |
| `hotel_lobby` | the front desk of the hotel across the airport forecourt: a long oak-fronted desk with a lowered accessible section, in front of a deep teal wall under a band of oak slats, the forecourt glass wall on the left and two lounge chairs on the right | wide, mid |
| `hotel_room` | a standard king room in the airport hotel, seen from the window corner: the bed against a tall deep teal upholstered headboard on the right, the entry passage between the wardrobe and the bathroom wall in the middle distance, and the room's entry door at the far end with its evacuation plan on the inside | wide, mid |
| `booking_desk` | an agent's desk in the travel company's team room on the second floor of the business-park office: a bench of desks with a deep teal felt screen, a calm teal felt wall with three plain-faced time-zone clocks, a wallboard, and a ribbon of windows looking out over the airfield | mid, wide |

**Cast**

- `travel_agent`: booking and duty-of-care agent at a desk with a headset and two monitors (changes, refunds, fraud checks, calls to stranded travellers)
- `team_lead`: the travel company's team lead, who comes to an agent's desk to help with an escalation or to coach
- `checkin_agent`: airport check-in agent (neutral uniform, no logo)
- `gate_agent`: gate agent at the podium (neutral uniform, no logo): boarding, cancellations, rebooking
- `cabin_crew`: cabin crew member (neutral uniform, no logo)
- `hotel_receptionist`: hotel receptionist behind the front desk (neutral uniform, no logo)
- `business_traveller`: business traveller with a carry-on
- `family`: a family of four with luggage
- `older_traveller`: an older traveller with a walking stick
- `backpacker`: a student backpacker
- `wheelchair_user`: a wheelchair user with an assistance staff member
- `travel_crowd`: airport crowd, full and receding
- `housekeeper`: hotel housekeeping attendant (neutral uniform, no logo) who enters guest rooms and notices welfare concerns

**Assets**

- **core (almost always needed):** suitcase (cabin and checked, blank tag); boarding pass (slots); passport (closed and open, slot); phone with booking screen (slot); headset; hotel key card in its sleeve; payment card (no brand); bag tag (blank, slot); itinerary document (slots); paper cup with lid; lanyard and badge; laptop
- **common:** luggage trolley; departure board (slots); queue post and belt; receipt (slots); clock with plain face (time zones); travel insurance document (slots); calendar (slots); world map (wall, no text); wheelchair; walking stick; neck pillow; two-way radio; evacuation plan (lines and arrows, no text)
- **optional:** baggage carousel; passport control booth; taxi or ride car (plain); train platform; cruise ship side view; hotel luggage cart; housekeeping trolley

## Warehouse and logistics (`warehouse`)

**For lessons on:** forklift and pedestrian safety, manual handling, picking accuracy, loading-dock safety, near-miss reporting, shift briefings and handover.

**Commonly made in:** flat 2D editorial, 2D story cartoon, isometric, 2.5D lit layers (not a ranking).

**The place:** A single-storey regional distribution centre on an edge-of-town business park in a temperate country, about ten years old, run by a wholesaler of household and hardware goods (boxed kettles and toasters, paint tins, garden tools, bedding) for about 120 people over two shifts. One steel portal-frame shed about 140 m long and 85 m deep, 12 m clear to the underside of the roof steel: twelve dock doors along the north wall, blocks of pallet racking in the middle, the packing and despatch area at the east end, and a two-storey block of offices and welfare rooms built inside the shed in the south-east corner, with the shift-briefing area in front of it.

**Scenes**

| id | what | shots |
|---|---|---|
| `aisle` | the end of a racking block on a main gangway: aisle mouths between tall racking runs on the left, a barrier-fenced walkway along the south wall on the right, and a zebra crossing joining them | wide, mid |
| `picking` | a 3 m wide working lane between two runs of pallet racking, where reach trucks put pallets away and pickers pick from the floor-level pallets, seen from inside the lane toward its bright open end at the main gangway | mid, wide |
| `loading_dock` | the inside of the dock face on the north wall: insulated dock doors in a row, the middle one open onto a trailer backed into its shelter with the leveller's lip resting on the trailer bed, the doors either side closed, marked lanes on the floor in front of each | wide, mid |
| `shift_board` | the fenced shift-briefing area on the shop floor in front of the two-storey welfare and office block, where a long board of the day's plan, the safety cross and near-miss cards hangs on the block's wall beside the shed's plain gable | wide, mid |

**Cast**

- `warehouse_operative`: warehouse operatives and pickers (hi-vis vest, safety boots, gloves, a wrist or handheld scanner)
- `forklift_driver`: forklift and reach-truck drivers (drawn with their truck props)
- `shift_lead`: shift lead or team leader with a radio and a tablet
- `dock_operative`: goods-in and goods-out dock operatives
- `driver`: a visiting lorry driver in their own hi-vis, kept to the drivers' waiting area and walkways
- `visitor_hi_vis`: a visitor in a visitor vest, escorted

**Assets**

- **core (almost always needed):** pallet racking bay (one bay, with beams, location labels and a load notice slot); Euro pallet (empty and loaded); cartons (open, closed, stacked, stretch-wrapped); counterbalance forklift (with a separate mast and forks); reach truck; hand pallet truck; handheld scanner (screen slot); hi-vis vest; safety boots and gloves; roll cage
- **common:** yellow pedestrian barrier; convex mirror; dock leveller, dock control panel and dock lamp; vehicle keys on a ring; shift briefing board (slots); radio; first-aid sign and kit; fire extinguisher and call point; spill kit; clipboard with a pick list (slot)
- **optional:** lorry and trailer (no livery); mobile steps; wheel chock; conveyor segment
