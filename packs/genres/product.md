# Genre: product

**Grounding posture:** strict. Every claim, number, quote, logo and badge traces to the product's own material (the
docs, the site, release notes, approved case studies) or to the product truth sheet that the owner approved. Nothing
is shown that the shipped product cannot do.

**Arc (in order; * = required):** hook* -> status quo* -> the shift* (name and one line) -> show it working* (2 or 3
value moments, the aha first) -> how it works -> proof* -> the ask* (one CTA and the end card, held 3 s or more)

**Opens with:** the viewer's moment or the result they want, readable with the sound off. Never a logo sting, a
company history or "Introducing".

**Good:**
- benefit said, then the feature doing it shown, then the result held;
- the product on screen early;
- one strong attributed proof;
- one ask;
- invented, consistent sample data;
- cutdown beats marked in the board.

**Bad:**
- a feature tour or a logo wall;
- busy dashboards behind narration that never points at anything;
- hype words instead of evidence;
- a menu of calls to action.

**Never:**
- an unsubstantiated performance or comparative claim;
- an altered quote or a testimonial implying atypical results without disclosure;
- an uncertified badge, or an unshipped feature shown as shipped;
- real customer data on screen;
- another company's interface or logo;
- a question for the viewer to answer, or a quiz.

**Other shapes (the person chooses; the arc above stays the default):**
- day in the life, before and after;
- aha first (a cold open on the result);
- feature launch (what is new, why it matters, where it lives);
- objection ladder (sales follow-up);
- technical evaluator (architecture, guarantees, integration, docs);
- internal rollout (why we are changing, what gets easier, when, where help is).

**Formats:**
- teaser 6 to 30 s;
- homepage 60 to 90 s;
- feature launch 30 to 60 s;
- demo 2 to 3 min;
- technical 2 to 4 min;
- internal rollout 60 to 120 s.

These lengths are conventions, not laws.

**Visual kinds that fit:**
- the product's own screens (real, cleaned or simplified, faithful);
- the request's path through the system;
- a before and after with the same person;
- numbers as results on the product's own screen.

**Story form that usually fits:** The viewer as the hero with the product as their tool; one running example (one
customer, one task) the whole film follows.

**Skill:** `skills/product/product-explainer` (the truth sheet, formats, arcs, showing software, honest claims, sound
off and cutdowns, voice, the review rubric).
